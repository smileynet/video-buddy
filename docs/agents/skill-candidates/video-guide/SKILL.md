# Video guide

Produce a useful learning path from the source. Use the reader's goal to choose the format; guide types, skills and pipeline stages are internal decisions.

## Project entry

Read the workspace's agent instructions and use its source-processing tools. In Video Buddy, follow the intake/enrichment and finalization contracts; mechanical extraction remains CLI-owned. Reuse existing artifacts before repeating acquisition. Preserve configured compute and private credential handling.

When the project has `docs/agents/video-guide-authoring.md`, read it before drafting. Use `docs/templates/video-guide.md` for structure and `docs/agents/video-guide-user-pathways.md` for the reader experience. Those documents own the project-specific rules; do not copy them into another skill or treat a general note prompt as permission to attribute outside research to the source.

## Author and revise

1. Recover enough of the whole source to understand its complete argument or procedure. Use relevant frames for settings, commands, brief states and visual decisions. A chapter list alone is not a teaching reference.
2. State what the reader can understand, decide or apply. Teach the source's worked example when it demonstrates one; otherwise explain the ideas and their application. Keep an additional proposed build or tool comparison in a separate, explicitly scoped companion.
3. Give the complete approach before section details. When broader context helps, research a short “Where this fits” explanation and a misconception-preventing distinction; put adjacent approaches behind an optional comparison. Mark descriptive labels as our framing. Research only what unblocks the current learning path.
4. Establish the reading hierarchy: takeaway, useful visual, action, observable check and immediate recovery. Disclose expanded rationale and provenance. Keep essential constraints and experimental status visible. Use semantic labels and restrained color together; source captures and explanatory diagrams have different evidence roles.
5. Where a route choice matters, recommend a named starting point and weigh relevant alternatives by fit, setup/upkeep and total cost. Respect hard constraints such as local handling. A conceptual lesson need not become tool shopping.
6. Label source content, researched additions, adaptations and actual execution distinctly. A model transcript/review is not human verification; a tested component is not a completed workflow; browser checks are not observed learning success.

## Publish and verify

Keep one canonical teaching fixture across comparison views. Preserve topic/evidence mapping, optional playback, separate example navigation, actual notes/persistence behavior and private responses. Follow the project's publishing contract for the canonical artifact.

Inspect desktop/mobile, keyboard disclosures, source links, visual readability and the skim path with optional panels closed. Check that required instructions and recovery remain usable. Report exactly what was executed and what the reader can review. Stop at the requested scope; unrelated research, a multi-source course or a knowledge platform is separate work.
