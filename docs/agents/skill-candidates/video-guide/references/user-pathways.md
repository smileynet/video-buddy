# Reader experience contract

Agent routing, evidence bookkeeping and CLI operations live in [authoring guidance](authoring.md). This document governs what readers see and do.

Start with the desired result and one useful next action. Infer the route: understand/apply an idea, try a demonstrated step or find an answer again. Internal guide types and skills are not onboarding choices.

For concepts use idea → example → application. For source walkthroughs use prerequisites → steps → checkpoints. Keep costs, hard constraints and missing dependencies visible before the reader invests effort. When constraints exclude the default, show the fitting route as the main recommendation.

Put the immediate recovery action beside each required check. Collapse deeper explanation and troubleshooting. Provide direct reference lookup, nearby evidence and a clear return to the topic.

Each optional branch shows its purpose, requirements, likely effort/cost, exact readiness and one next action. Link back to the exact originating topic. Retain work where supported and explain whether it survives navigation, reload or closing. An open original tab is not crash recovery.

Use plain labels: “In the video”, “For your setup”, “Added starting point”. Explain what added help supplies and what remains. Keep internal tickets, model receipts and authoring machinery behind maintainer references. A reader can finish the lesson without installing tools or opening benchmarks.

Validate these tasks: understand in ten minutes without setup; try one passage; recover from failure; respect local-only audio; retrieve a command; handle a missing creator program. Distinguish comprehension, navigation, execution and preference. A document check does not establish reader success.

## Read at two depths

A reader can skim the outcome, topic takeaway, useful visual, next action and success check. A reader who needs explanation can expand a specifically named callout without losing the action. Keep essential prerequisites, risk to the current work, experimental status and immediate recovery in the skim path. Use captioned screengrabs for visual decisions and diagrams for relationships; source moments and originals remain reachable. Text equivalents keep the guide usable without the image. Dense text moved wholesale behind a generic “More” panel is not a clear hierarchy.

Before section navigation, show the whole approach in a brief synthesis and a small map: what the ideas add up to, how they connect and where the important limits are. The reader should know what they are learning before following its parts. Keep the full explanation in the relevant sections; avoid recreating the video's suspense or turning the opening into another wall of text.

Separate the opening's roles visually: “Where this fits” gives category and relationships; “The whole approach at a glance” supplies the source synthesis. Related-approach comparison expands on demand. Use labeled action/check/recovery blocks with consistent, restrained color and readable contrast. Keep the selected topic and immediate next action easy to find without opening the complete topic index.

Try, Check and If stuck blocks are expandable help: the closed summary remains a useful immediate action, criterion or recovery step. Opening reveals concrete topic-specific instructions and troubleshooting. Readers can use the summaries alone or expand the part they need; keyboard operation and A/B topic/open-state continuity remain available within the page.
